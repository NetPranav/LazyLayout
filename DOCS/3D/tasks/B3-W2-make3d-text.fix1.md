Do not run anything; reason from this evidence (I ran the code):
- `extrudeRings([L], {depth: 10, bevel: {size: 1, segments: 2, profile: "round"}})` for an L-shaped outline (0,0)-(20,0)-(20,10)-(10,10)-(10,30)-(0,30): 44 vertices, 74 triangles, 10 bad edges, ALL OF THEM at the outline at z = 1 (the full-size outline ring at the bottom of the wall, e.g. edge (0,0,1)-(20,0,1) used by only ONE triangle) and at z = 9 (top). So the side wall's ends and the bevel rings do not share vertices/edges where the bevel meets the straight wall (the wall starts at z=1 but the bevel's last ring is a separate, unshared copy, or the wall quad strip is built from a different ring subdivision than the bevel's). With bevel size 0 the same L is perfectly watertight (12 vertices, 0 bad edges). Square and circle with bevel pass; the L, rounded rectangle, star, ring with hole, crescent, glyphs fail: the failing shapes are those where `offsetRings(rings, -size)` (the inset ring) has a DIFFERENT NUMBER OF VERTICES than the original outline (the inset removes or adds vertices at concave corners and with round joins), while the wall and bevel strips pair vertex i of one ring with vertex i of the next. Fix: build the bevel by moving EACH original vertex along its own inward bisector (vertex-for-vertex offset: `p + n_bisector * size / cos(half angle)` clamped by a mitre limit) so that every bevel ring has exactly the original vertex count and strips pair up one to one; use `offsetRings` only for the cap contour if you need real geometry, and make sure the cap contour's vertices are the SAME vertices as the bevel's last ring (shared indices, or weld at the end with `weldVertices`: make sure it is actually applied to the final mesh and that its tolerance relates to the model size, not 1e-4 only).
- `inflateRings([L], {depth: 5, resolution: 8})`: 75 vertices, 128 triangles, 25 bad edges including four triangles sharing one edge ((0,22.5,0)-(2.5,30,0) used 4 times) and one-sided edges along the outline at z in (0..1.5). The front and back pillow surfaces are triangulated from a grid or ring set that does not match at the outline, and the surface is triangulated twice in places. A simple robust design: build the pillow from the SAME flattened contour at the rim (height 0), then concentric inset rings (use vertex-for-vertex bisector offsets as above, for k = 1..resolution, heights from `sdfToHeight`), triangulate only the innermost ring with `triangulate()`, connect ring k to ring k+1 with a strip, and mirror it for the back; join front and back at the rim by sharing the rim vertices.
